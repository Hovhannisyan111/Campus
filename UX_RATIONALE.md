# UX & Design Notes: IES Applicant Assistant

---

### Why I Wanted to Build This

When students search for scholarships abroad, they usually face two main problems:

1. **Way too much text:** Most scholarship websites are full of long, formal descriptions. A student usually just wants quick answers to two simple questions: *"What will I actually be doing?"* and *"Do my skills fit this programme?"*
2. **Missing the community aspect:** Most people assume an exchange semester is only about going to lectures and taking exams. But IES is different because it includes **hands-on community engagement and real student contribution**. If applicants do not see this right away, they might think it is just a normal study program or assume they are not qualified.

I wanted to make a clean, friendly web tool that helps students understand what makes IES special in under a minute, test their fit, and head to the official website with confidence.

---

### What I Kept in Mind About Students

* **Most people browse on their phones:** Students usually find links on Instagram, TikTok, or messaging group chats. If a website looks cluttered or takes too long to explain its point, they leave. That is why I designed everything for mobile first.
* **A short quiz is much less intimidating than a long PDF:** Answering 4 simple questions feels easy, low-pressure, and engaging. It gives students a quick idea of where they stand.
* **Clear next steps:** When someone finishes the check, they immediately see which contribution area suits them best (like Technology, Design, or Media) and get a direct button to the official Copernicus Berlin portal.

---

### Design Choices

* **Brand Colors:** I took the colors directly from the Copernicus Berlin logo:
  * **Orange (`#F37021`):** friendly and energetic, represents youth and student ideas.
  * **Navy (`#1A2A44`):** gives the page an academic, trustworthy feel.
  * **Soft cream background (`#FDFBF7`):** much warmer and easier on the eyes than pure white.
* **Simple typography:** I chose `Inter` because it is clean, modern, and very easy to read on mobile screens.
* **Step-by-step questions:** Instead of showing all questions at once, the tool shows them one by one. This keeps the screen clean and helps the student focus.
* **Honest disclaimers:** Because this is an informal guide, I made sure every result screen clearly states:  
  *“This tool is for informational guidance only and does not determine official eligibility. Please check the official Copernicus Berlin portal before applying.”*

---

### How the Flow Works

```text
01 Discover  → Read the core message: "Study abroad is only the beginning."
     ↓
02 Explore   → See the 3 pillars: Study, Community, and Engagement.
     ↓
03 Check     → Answer 4 quick questions (enrollment, Berlin motivation, teamwork, skills).
     ↓
04 Learn     → Get matched with contribution areas and read common FAQ questions.
     ↓
05 Apply     → Direct link to the official application guidelines on copernicusberlin.org/en.
```

---

### What Success Looks Like for This Tool

If Copernicus Berlin used a tool like this on their website, my goals would be:
* More students completing the self-check instead of dropping off on long text pages.
* Better prepared applicants who already know that IES is about contributing practical skills, not just attending classes.
* Fewer repetitive emails to the Copernicus team because common initial questions are already answered in the FAQ.
