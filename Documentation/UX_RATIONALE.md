# UX & Design Case Study: IES Applicant Assistant

---

### Why I Built This Tool

When university students explore international scholarships, they usually run into two main problems:

1. **Information overload:** Most scholarship pages are full of dense legal and institutional text. A student usually wants to know two basic things right away: *"What will I actually be doing?"* and *"Is this programme right for someone with my skills?"*
2. **Missing the community focus:** Most students assume that an exchange semester simply means attending lectures in another country. But the International Excellence Scholarship (IES) is different: it combines **study abroad with active community engagement and student contribution**. If this isn't obvious within the first few seconds, students might overlook the programme or think it's only for people with perfect theoretical grades.

I wanted to design a simple, friendly web tool that solves this by giving prospective applicants an easy, 4-step way to explore the programme, check their alignment, and get directed to the official Copernicus Berlin website.

---

### Key Observations & User Behavior

When designing the flow, I thought about how students actually discover opportunities today:

* **Short attention span on mobile:** Most students find links through Instagram, TikTok, or WhatsApp group chats. If a page doesn't explain the main value within 15 seconds, they close the tab.
* **Low-pressure interaction:** Opening a long application PDF feels intimidating. Taking a quick 60-second interactive questionnaire is fun, low-pressure, and encouraging.
* **Clear next steps:** Students want to know where they stand. After completing the check, the tool immediately suggests relevant contribution tracks (like IT, graphic design, or communications) and links them to the official application guidelines.

---

### Design Decisions

* **Colours:** I used Copernicus Berlin’s brand colours:
  * **Orange (`#F37021`):** energetic and approachable, showing youth and student initiative.
  * **Navy (`#1A2A44`):** grounds the design in European academic credibility.
  * **Soft cream background (`#FDFBF7`):** much friendlier and easier to read on screens than harsh stark white.
* **Typography:** Clean, modern sans-serif (`Inter`) for readability across all screen sizes.
* **Mobile-first:** The buttons, cards, and quiz steps are designed for thumbs and phone screens first.
* **Honest disclaimers:** Because this is an informal student tool, I made sure every result screen clearly says:  
  *“This tool is for informational guidance only and does not determine official eligibility. Please check the official Copernicus Berlin portal before applying.”*

---

### How the Journey Works

```text
01 Discover     → See the core message: "Study abroad is only the beginning."
      ↓
02 Explore      → Learn about the 3 pillars: Study, Community, and Engagement.
      ↓
03 Check        → Answer 4 quick questions (enrollment, Berlin motivation, community interest, skills).
      ↓
04 Learn        → View matched contribution areas and read common questions in the FAQ.
      ↓
05 Apply        → One-click jump to the official application guidelines on copernicusberlin.org/en.
```

---

### What Success Looks Like

If this tool were deployed publicly by Copernicus Berlin, here is what I would look for:
* **Higher completion rate:** More students finishing the self-check compared to reading a long PDF.
* **Better prepared applicants:** Students reading the official instructions already knowing that IES expects them to contribute their skills, not just sit in lectures.
* **Fewer repetitive support questions:** The FAQ and orientation tool answer the most common initial doubts.
