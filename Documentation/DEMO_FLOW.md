# How I Would Present This Project (4-Minute Walkthrough)

---

### Step 1: The Core Problem (Approx. 30 seconds)
* **What I would explain:**  
  When university students first see an international scholarship page, they usually think of a traditional exchange semester—attending lectures in another country. But IES is special because of its **Community Engagement** pillar. I built the **IES Applicant Assistant** as a lightweight web app that communicates this core difference in 15 seconds, tests whether the student is a good match, and directs them to the official application.
* **What I show on screen:**  
  The top hero section with the tagline: *“Study abroad is only the beginning.”* and the Copernicus Berlin branding.

---

### Step 2: The Three Pillars (Approx. 45 seconds)
* **What I would explain:**  
  Students don't like reading walls of text. I organised the programme into three clear pillars: **Academic Study**, **International Community**, and **Community Engagement**. This makes it immediately clear that scholars contribute real skills—whether in coding, design, media, or research.
* **What I show on screen:**  
  The three pillar cards and the 5-step applicant journey (Discover → Explore → Check → Learn → Apply).

---

### Step 3: The Interactive Fit Checker (Approx. 90 seconds)
* **What I would explain:**  
  This is the main feature of the prototype. Instead of reading through formal rules, a student answers four simple questions:
  1. Are you currently enrolled in a university?
  2. What is your motivation for studying in Berlin?
  3. Are you excited to volunteer and work in an international team?
  4. Which skills do you bring? (e.g., Technology & Coding, Graphic Design, Communications, Research).
* **What I show on screen:**  
  Clicking through the 4 steps, submitting, and showing the results screen. I would highlight the clear recommendation: *“Your skills match our Technology & Digital Innovation track!”* and point out the disclaimer reminding students to check the official guidelines before applying.

---

### Step 4: Answering Questions & FAQ (Approx. 45 seconds)
* **What I would explain:**  
  I added a searchable FAQ section categorised by topics (General, Community, Application). I also included a prototype for an information assistant that answers basic questions using only verified data, safely reminding students to consult the official portal for cycle-specific dates and funding details.
* **What I show on screen:**  
  The categorized FAQ accordion and the clean contact section.

---

### Step 5: How It's Built (Approx. 30 seconds)
* **What I would explain:**  
  The app is built with **React 18, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS**. What's important is that **all text, questions, and links are kept in separate data files** (`src/data/`). That means Copernicus Berlin team members could easily update dates or questions in the future without having to write or touch any code.
* **Closing:**  
  Thank the reviewers and invite questions about the UX decisions or technical setup.
